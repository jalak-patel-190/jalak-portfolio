Case 04 · Reading the tracker

About fifty accounts, same dates, honest comparison

The same accounts compared at the same dates, plus an honest look at what happened after I left.

Context

A tracker is only useful if the dates match

I maintained a tracker across YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and other platforms. The comparison below uses the same accounts at two fixed dates so growth is attributable and not mixed with new accounts that appeared later.

Scale

About fifty accounts, 15 December to 1 July

Combined reach across the compared set moved from 529,763 to 637,163 — a +20.3% change on the same accounts. YouTube, Facebook and Instagram each grew; the table on the hub shows the platform split.

After I left

What the public numbers show later

Public channel numbers after my tenure are shown where available so the case does not stop at the day I left. Internal files such as the tracker itself are not published; the numbers are shown, the files are not.

What this showed

Same-account growth is the clean story

Comparing the same accounts at the same dates avoids inflating growth with new properties. That is the standard this case holds to.
