READING THE TRACKER

Fifty accounts cannot be judged by feel. This case covers the tracker I maintained, what it showed during my year, and what it showed after I left.

+20.3% same accounts50 accounts8 months of data

Context

THE TRACKER I MAINTAINED

The team logged subscribers and followers for about fifty accounts every day, one sheet per month, and I maintained that tracker. It covers December 2022 to December 2023, so it holds the last eight months of my year and six months after it.

I cross-checked it against the company’s official analytics report of 19 June 2023. The report lists the main channel at 346,911 subscribers, and the tracker shows 346,075 for mid June, within a quarter of a percent.

Problem

A TOTAL CAN MISLEAD

With about fifty accounts, a portfolio total can rise or fall just because accounts were added, renamed or dropped. A fair read needs the same accounts at both ends.

Strategy

SAME ACCOUNTS, SAME DATES, THEN CROSS-CHECK

Every comparison here follows three rules.

Same accounts at both dates
Outsourced and others’ channels excluded
Cross-checked against the official report

Outsourced accounts and channels run by other team members are not counted, because they were not my work.

Results

+20.3% ON THE SAME 47 ACCOUNTS

Between 15 December 2022 and 1 July 2023, the 47 accounts present at both dates grew from 529,763 to 637,163 subscribers and followers, a gain of 107,400. Facebook grew fastest at 43.0%, YouTube added the most in absolute terms at 73,354, and Instagram grew 17.0%.

These are results of the team’s portfolio during the months I worked on it, not numbers I claim alone.

What the data shows. The main channel and the Shorts channels sped up after July 2023, and the regional long form channels slowed down.
What it may suggest. This is an interpretation, not proof. Momentum on the flagship and Shorts channels may have kept compounding after the handoff, while channels that depend on continuous cultural and keyword judgment lost pace without it.

One more number belongs here. The tracker also logs views earned in the first 48 hours on the main channel. They averaged 43,590 over my year, about 5% below the 46,095 of the two months before I joined. They dipped from 50,269 (July to December 2022) to 36,822 (January to June 2023), recovered to 43,581 by June, and averaged 76,140 after July 2023. These numbers do not support a claim of growth during my year, and I do not make one.

What this showed

SCALE IS A SYSTEMS PROBLEM

A portfolio of about fifty accounts only works if every audience has its own vocabulary, its own product mix and its own reason to trust the seller, and if the team has a shared way to track all of it. I learned to read the data per channel instead of per portfolio, to separate what the team built from what I built, and to say clearly which is which.

What this case does not claim
  • It does not claim I designed the language map, the title sheet or the keyword roster. My team leader led them and I worked inside them.
  • It does not claim the audience growth as mine alone.
  • It does not claim audience results for the founder’s personal pages.
  • It does not count outsourced accounts or channels run by other team members.
  • It covers the last eight months of my year, because that is where the tracker starts.
