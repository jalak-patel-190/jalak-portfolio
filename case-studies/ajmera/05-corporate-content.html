Case 05 · Ajmera Fashion

FROM OFFICE ACTIVITIES TO COMPANY CONTENT

Turning employee activities, festivals and office moments into social content for a corporate audience, with HR support and creative production coordination.

8 public Instagram examples 3 content occasions 1 corporate content track

01 · The context

THE COMPANY HAD MOMENTS WORTH SHOWING

My work at Ajmera Fashion was not limited to product-led social media. I also supported HR and the company's corporate Instagram content.

The purpose was different from the regional product channels. This content was mainly about employees, company culture and the moments happening inside the organisation.

That included occasions such as Diwali, Maha Shivratri and team activities. Instead of treating these as internal events that disappeared after the day was over, they could also become part of how the company showed its people and culture publicly.

02 · The problem

AN OFFICE EVENT IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY GOOD CONTENT

Corporate content can easily become a record of what happened: a few photographs, a festival greeting, a group picture, then nothing more.

The content needed a different question behind it:

How do we turn an internal company moment into a social piece that feels like content, rather than documentation?

That meant thinking about the people in the moment, what the activity communicated about the workplace, how it should be presented, and what format made sense for the platform.

03 · The strategic decision

TREAT CULTURE AS CONTENT, NOT JUST EVENT COVERAGE

I approached the corporate page differently from the product-focused channels.

The content centred on:

  • Employees and team participation
  • Office activities and shared moments
  • Festivals celebrated inside the company
  • Informal workplace interactions
  • Moments that gave an outside audience a sense of the people behind the company

The idea was simple: the company did not only have products to show. It also had people and moments that could make the organisation feel more human.

05 · The work in practice

THREE TYPES OF MOMENTS BECAME CONTENT

Festivals

Diwali and Maha Shivratri gave the corporate page occasions to show the company participating in cultural moments alongside its employees. The content was not built around selling a product. It was built around participation and company identity.

Team activities

Team activities created opportunities to show employees interacting outside their usual work routine. These moments helped make the page about the people inside the company, not only the business it operates.

Office culture

Other office moments could be turned into short-form social pieces when there was something worth showing. The focus stayed on people, participation and the experience of being part of the organisation.

One of the strongest examples of this approach is the public Life at Ajmera office reel.

The content decision: an office activity can become a public story about the people and culture behind the business.

08 · Production approach

I DID NOT HAVE TO DESIGN EVERYTHING MYSELF

Good social production is not the same as personally touching every file.

For these corporate posts, I worked with professional editors and graphic support when required. My responsibility was to help make sure the original activity translated into a usable social asset: the idea, the content direction, the coordination, the production flow and the final publishing.

That distinction matters because the work was collaborative. I was part of the creative production process without claiming to be the sole designer or editor.

09 · The result

THE OUTPUT WAS A CORPORATE CONTENT TRACK

This case does not have a verified post-level performance metric, so I am not attaching a reach, engagement or follower-growth number to it.

The documented outcome was the creation and management of corporate social content around:

  • Employee and team activities
  • Diwali and Maha Shivratri celebrations
  • Office and workplace moments
  • HR-supported company activities

I was involved in planning, coordination, creative direction, production coordination and publishing, with professional editing and graphic support where needed.

10 · What this changed

SOCIAL MEDIA DID NOT HAVE TO SELL SOMETHING EVERY TIME

This work expanded how I thought about company content.

A product post has an obvious subject: the product. A corporate post has to find its subject in people, moments, behaviour and culture.

That means asking different questions:

  • What happened inside the company that is actually worth showing?
  • Who should be part of the story?
  • What does this moment say about the workplace?
  • Should it be a photo, graphic, reel or another format?
  • How can the content feel like a social post rather than an internal notice?

That was the useful lesson from this work: not every piece of brand content needs to sell something. Some content can show who is behind the business.

12 · The takeaway

PEOPLE CAN BE PART OF THE BRAND STORY

At Ajmera Fashion, corporate activities gave the company another kind of content to publish. Employees, festivals and office moments became material for showing the organisation from the inside.

For me, the important shift was from “an event happened” to “there is a story here worth turning into content.”

People → Culture → Content
Internal moments can become public-facing brand content when the story, format and production are considered deliberately.
