Case Study

AJMERA FASHION
31 CHANNELS.
619,539 SUBSCRIBERS.
ONE REGIONAL STRATEGY.

Turning language variation into a 31-channel distribution system, and being honest about what happened to it after I left.

Role Digital Marketing Executive Duration Jul 2022 to Jul 2023 Scope YouTube, Regional Strategy, Content Systems
01 / THE CONTEXT
What was happening?

Ajmera Fashion is a B2B textile wholesaler whose real business is helping small retailers and entrepreneurs start their own clothing businesses. YouTube was not their revenue engine, inventory sales were. But YouTube was how they built trust and reached buyers in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities before those buyers ever placed an order.

This was my first job in digital marketing. No prior experience, no existing playbook. The company already had a large, loosely organized set of YouTube channels split by language, by product category, and by business model. The opportunity was not simply to publish more content. It was to find the actual audience logic underneath a channel portfolio that had grown without one.

02 / THE PROBLEM
REGIONAL AUDIENCES ARE NOT LANGUAGE AUDIENCES.

A video uploaded in Marathi does not automatically reach a Marathi audience if the product, the keywords, and the framing do not match what that specific region actually searches for. The channels existed. The regional logic connecting them to their audience did not.

The challenge was not producing more content. It was deciding what each region actually wanted, and building a system that could scale that judgment across more than 30 channels rather than one at a time.

03 / THE STRATEGIC DECISION
DON'T TRANSLATE CONTENT. TRANSLATE THE STRATEGY.

Instead of treating language as a translation problem, I treated each region as its own audience with its own vocabulary, its own product preference, and its own reason to trust a seller. A single national campaign copy-pasted across six languages was the default. I built the opposite of that.

06 / WHY IT WORKED
Not every channel needed to perform the same job.

Language channels reached regional buyers who trusted their own dialect and product terms. Educational content converted casual viewers into people who could picture themselves as sellers. Brand ambassador content and CEO features built the trust a purely transactional channel never earns.

The strategy was therefore not "make more videos." It was "give each regional audience a reason that actually applies to them."

07 / THE HONEST NUMBER
Growth rate more than doubled after I left. Here's the actual breakdown.

I left this role in July 2023. Tracking data continues six months past that date. Restricted to the 19 channels with continuous coverage on both sides of that line, so the comparison isn't distorted by channels that simply stopped being tracked, portfolio growth rate went from 328 subscribers a day during my tenure to 685 a day after.

I'm not going to hide that number or spin it. But it's misleading left on its own, so here's what's actually underneath it.

Shorts channels, faster Product channels, flat Regional long-form, slower
Shorts +24.1, Product +0.8, Regional -7.7

Main channel and Gujarati Shorts drove most of the acceleration, Main went from roughly 191 to 419 subscribers a day, Gujarati Shorts from 21 to 123. At the same time, regional long-form channels that depend on ongoing cultural and keyword localization slowed down. Telugu specifically fell from roughly 37 a day to 18, more than halving.

08 / THE DATA

Subscriber counts and 48 hour view velocity, tracked daily across the channel portfolio, December 2022 through December 2023. Every figure here is a direct calculation from that raw data, not an estimate.

10 / WHAT THIS CHANGED
The lesson wasn't that growth continued. It was where it continued.

Two entirely new channels, Telugu 2 and Lehenga, launched from zero during this window and had real subscriber bases by the time tracking ends. Momentum on the flagship and Shorts-format channels kept compounding after I left. Momentum on the regional long-form channels, the ones that depend on continuous hands-on cultural judgment, did not.

That changed how I think about content systems generally. Some parts of a system are self-sustaining once built. Others need a person still paying attention.
