Primary case · Swad

TWO CHANNELS, ONE RETENTION SYSTEM

Institute and owner channels grown in the same year through series formats, talent-fit content, and a clear view of where views stop turning into revenue.

23.44M+ combined views +141.9K subscribers 1.43M+ watch hours ₹16.47L+ est. revenue

Context

INSTITUTE BRAND PLUS OWNER CHANNEL

Swad Cooking Institute needed content that supported course discovery and trust. Alongside it sat Chef Sneha Thakkar Recipes, a personal IP channel handed over with roughly 4,000 to 5,000 subscribers and only three videos averaging about 15K views each.

The job was to grow both without treating them as the same product.

Swad Cooking · Sneha Thakkar Recipes

Problem

VIEWS WITHOUT A SYSTEM DO NOT COMPOUND

Live sessions could draw same-day attention and still die as assets. Long recordings full of off-topic chat do not get rewatched. Novel recipes alone did not beat familiar topics reframed with one new fact. And watch hours did not automatically become revenue without a format and membership strategy behind them.

Strategy

MATCH FORMAT TO FUNNEL AND TALENT

Series formats
Talent topic-fit
Student as proof
Revenue levers
  • Familiar topics with a twist. Series such as Monday Mistake and storage explainers, built on one new fact about something people already cook.
  • Talent topic-fit. Content matched to each chef’s face-value and strength, not a single house style forced on everyone.
  • Students as marketing assets. Diploma and basic course content that put real learners on camera for lead generation and trust.
  • Revenue diagnosis. Membership and format choices aimed at the gap between watch hours and monetisation.

Execution

WHAT RAN DURING THE YEAR

Flagship diploma and basic course content

Diploma enrollments were observed to come heavily from Instagram and YouTube, so live sessions with current students were used to grow that funnel. Basic cooking content used shareable, lighter formats for reach.

Monday Mistake series

A weekly series on common kitchen mistakes, built on the rule that viewers often need one new fact about a familiar dish more than they need a brand-new recipe.

Chef Sneha Thakkar Recipes

From a near-empty channel at handoff to about 50,000 subscribers and 7.38M+ views by the end of the tenure, with the growth curve inside a single year.

Livestreams, ad films, and production

Ongoing livestream output, directed pieces, and short-form R&D for original series. Technical failures were treated as recovery problems with a publishable outcome where possible, not only as downtime.

Farewell Diwali marathon

A multi-hour Diwali livestream with a large recipe menu and multiple chefs, organised as a close to the tenure rather than as a growth campaign.

Results

GROWTH WITH A CLEAR REVENUE LESSON

Combined views crossed 23.44M+ with more than 141.9K subscribers added across both channels. The owner channel alone moved from a few thousand subscribers to about 50K and 7.38M+ views inside the year.

Watch hours on long lives still exposed a monetisation gap: high same-day engagement did not equal durable, rewatchable assets or proportional ad revenue. Membership was introduced as one lever; the deeper fix is format design so content keeps earning after the live moment ends.

What this case does not claim
  • It does not claim company course revenue beyond the role of content in the discovery funnel.
  • Estimated YouTube revenue is platform-side, not a full business P&L.
  • Individual series and ad films will get separate deep-dive pages when the polished case set is ready.

What this showed

RETENTION IS A FORMAT DECISION

Familiar topics with one new fact outperformed novelty for its own sake. Talent has a topic-fit, not only a follower count. Watch hours without a content-format strategy leave revenue on the table. And how a team closes a chapter matters as much as how it performs mid-year.
