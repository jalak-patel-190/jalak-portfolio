The Company

Cinekorn Entertainment is a Mumbai based film distribution, production, and content syndication company, incorporated in January 2010 under founders Kalapi Vinit Nagada and Ilachi Vinit Nagada. The business runs theatrical releases, satellite distribution, and OTT syndication across Bollywood, South Indian, regional Gujarati and Marathi, and Hollywood titles, alongside a significant digital footprint anchored by the Cinekorn Movies YouTube channel.

I joined as Senior Social Media Manager, holding direct responsibility for digital movie content strategy, revenue generation across platforms, and LinkedIn accounts for both the company and its founder. What follows is a portfolio strategy applied across nine live accounts, not a single channel playbook. That distinction matters for the role I am building toward. A Creative Director does not own one channel well, they design a system that holds together across many.

CASE 01

Solving a Data Bottleneck With AI Assisted Tooling

Early in this role I was handed a task well outside standard content work. Three to four files, each holding 1,000 to 2,000 rows, needed sorting by tier classification. That is 6,000 to 8,000 rows of manual categorisation sitting between me and actual strategy work.

I opened Google Colab, used Gemini to generate the Python needed to automate the sort, and closed the task out in two days.

The point of including this isn't the code. It's the instinct. I had already built fluency with AI tools before joining Cinekorn, and I integrate that fluency into how I execute everywhere, not as a novelty but as a default. Knowing which tool collapses a multi day task into an afternoon is a strategist's judgment call, not an engineer's. That judgment is the same one a Creative Director needs when deciding where a team's time actually belongs.

CASE 02

Telugu and Kannada, Proof of Judgment Under Constraint

Not every channel in a portfolio starts with a strong hand. Telugu and Kannada arrived with the weakest source material I manage, low resolution prints, thin catalogs, and in Kannada's case, a channel history where the majority of historical views trace back to content that no longer exists in the library, almost certainly removed under a copyright claim before my time.

The Quality Workaround

When source footage is capped at 480p, presenting it as premium content invites unfavourable comparison and viewer drop off. Instead, I reframed the positioning entirely. Titles and descriptions lean into language like old movies, cult classic, and classic cinema, setting viewer expectation before they click rather than disappointing it after. Thumbnail quality gets toned down to match, and on the lowest quality uploads I skip a custom thumbnail altogether, because a Tier 3 audience responds to authenticity signals differently than a Tier 1 audience does.

Cinekorn Telugu
Zero recorded activity of any kind before 2026. Every one of its 3,516 lifetime views comes from the 31 videos I published after taking the channel from a complete standstill.

Cinekorn Kannada
78,782 lifetime views, but 71,357 of those sit on content published before 2026 that is no longer in the current library. The clean, attributable number is 7,425 views across 98 videos I published from a rebuilt base.

Case 4: Cinekorn Gujarati, A Cross Platform Distribution System

The System

Cinekorn Gujarati does not run out of content the way most teams think it does. I built a distribution architecture where one licensed film gets deliberately engineered into three to six separate content products, each matched to a specific platform's audience behaviour, so the same asset earns multiple independent lives instead of one release.

This is not repetition, it is audience segmentation applied to content strategy. YouTube carries the primary audience, viewers who came to watch a full film, so that release gets full SEO treatment across Hindi, English, and regional keyword sets. Facebook skews 35 and older, concentrated in Tier 3 cities, and the audience there wants something to watch on a phone more than it wants a specific format, so a three minute cut performs the same job a full film would. Instagram is a scroll audience, so the job there is one hooked scene, not the story.

Because each platform draws a genuinely different viewer by age, city tier, and intent, releasing the same IP across all three multiplies reach instead of splitting it.

Rebuilding the Facebook Page From Zero

Cinekorn Gujarati's Facebook page was restricted by Meta. Its last post before the restriction was May 18, 2025. Its next post was March 25, 2026, a ten month blackout with zero output.

WindowPostsTotal ViewsViews per Day
2025, pre-restriction, 11 active weeks6526,325,860333,239
2026, post-recovery, 176 days43347,351,230270,578

Meta's own dashboard reports this as a 41 percent decline year over year. That framing is technically accurate and strategically misleading, and I want to be precise about why rather than simply dispute it. The 2025 baseline was not a steady, healthy year. It was an eleven week hot streak where one post carried 17 percent of all views and three posts carried nearly a third of the total, followed immediately by a ten month collapse. Comparing a sustained 176 day, 433 post rebuild against a short viral spike is not a fair year over year read.

The honest claim, the one I will defend in any interview, is this. I took a dead page and rebuilt it to 6.7 times its prior posting volume, sustained across six straight months. The 2025 run had higher intensity while it lasted. What it did not have was consistency, and consistency is the harder thing to engineer.

The Ventilator Funnel

Inside the recovery window, I ran the same distribution logic against a second licensed film, Ventilator, splitting it into 39 parts released April 20 through May 28, generating 4,015,837 views. Several of these parts became the page's top earning content in that window.

One tactic came directly out of this campaign. When viewers commented asking what film a clip was from or where to watch it complete, I answered personally with the YouTube link to the full version, turning the Facebook comment section into a manual redirect channel feeding the platform where full length, ad supported viewing actually lives. That is not community management, that is cross platform funnel design.

What I Am Still Diagnosing

Two open items, stated plainly, because owning the gaps as directly as the wins is part of the job.

  • The July to August drop. Average views per post fell from roughly 104,000 to 23,000, and posting volume roughly halved, well after the restriction had lifted. This is not the blackout resurfacing. Something in the format, cadence, or platform behaviour shifted, and identifying which one is my next priority on this account.
  • The restriction cause. I do not yet have a confirmed explanation for why Meta flagged the page originally. I will have one ready before this goes in front of anyone who asks.

The strategic principle. A single piece of IP is not one asset, it is raw material for as many audience relationships as you are willing to architect around it. Most operators treat a film as a single release event. I treat it as a portfolio decision, which formats, which platforms, which sequencing extracts the most total audience value from one piece of licensed content. That is the difference between running a channel and running a content system, and it is the exact muscle a Creative Director role requires.

What This Role Is Building Toward

One IP, many audiences

A film is not a single release, it is raw material for as many format and platform relationships as the audience data supports.

Legacy performance is not your performance

A channel total that includes content you did not touch is a number that collapses the moment someone asks you to defend it. Separating the two, every time, is what makes the rest of the portfolio trustworthy.

A percentage without context is a trap

A 41 percent year over year decline sounds damning until you know the prior year was an eleven week viral spike followed by a ten month blackout. Knowing when to interrogate a metric instead of reporting it is strategic judgment, not spin.

Consistency is the harder skill

Anyone can catch a viral post. Rebuilding a dead page to 6.7 times its prior volume and holding that pace for six months is systems thinking applied to content operations.

Own the gaps as directly as the wins

The August drop on Facebook does not have an answer yet. Saying so, and naming it as an active priority, is what separates a strategist from someone reporting a highlight reel.

Designing Systems, Not Just Managing Channels

This portfolio spans nine accounts across three platforms, a legacy catalog, two rebuilt channels, and two LinkedIn presences, held together by one distribution logic applied consistently. That is the operating range I am bringing to a Creative Strategist role today, and the foundation I am building toward Creative Director.
