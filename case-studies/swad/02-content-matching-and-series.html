MATCHING CONTENT TO THE CHEF, AND THE MONDAY MISTAKE SERIES

Familiar topics told with one new fact outperformed novel recipes. That single insight built a how-to-store series and a weekly mistakes series.

Case 03 · Context

THE CHEFS ON CAMERA HAD DIFFERENT STRENGTHS

Spending time with the chef team showed that each chef carried different on-camera strengths. The owner, Chef Sneha, drove higher views when she taught authentic recipes or cooking tips.

Case 03 · Execution

MATCH THE TOPIC TO THE CHEF, THEN TO THE VIEWER

Analyse each chef’s strength
Match topics and recipes to it
Take familiar topics
Add one new fact or method
Build a series

Case 04 · Context

A WEEKLY SERIES ON THE MISTAKES EVERYONE MAKES

The same principle, a familiar topic with a new angle, became a weekly series on common kitchen mistakes that most home cooks do not realise they make.

Case 04 · Execution

MONDAY MISTAKE

Each Monday, one everyday kitchen mistake was explained. The audience responded strongly, and the format became a regular part of the channel.

▶Monday Mistake series

What this showed

NOVELTY IS NOT WHAT VIEWERS WANT

They want one new fact about something they think they already know. The series that followed from that insight were the easiest to plan and the most consistent to publish.
