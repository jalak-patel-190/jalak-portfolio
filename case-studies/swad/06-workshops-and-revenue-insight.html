Cases 10 and 11 · Swad Cooking Institute

WORKSHOP OPERATIONS, AND THE WATCH HOURS VERSUS REVENUE GAP

I ran the workshop back end each month, and diagnosed why daily livestreams were not turning into revenue. Edited uploads were the fix, but leadership reversed course too early.

Case 10 · Context

THE WORKSHOPS NEEDED A BACK END

Each new month began with the workshop schedule. I managed the live workshop calendar and the operations behind every free and paid YouTube workshop.

Case 10 · Execution

MONTHLY WORKSHOP OPERATIONS

Plan the month's workshops → Schedule each session → Run the back end on the day → Close the month and plan the next.

Case 11 · Context

WATCH HOURS WERE HIGH, BUT REVENUE WAS NOT FOLLOWING

After months of work, one problem stayed open. Watch hours were strong, but estimated revenue did not scale with them. I launched a YouTube membership as a new revenue lever, and the gap between watch hours and revenue remained.

Case 11 · Diagnosis

THE LIVESTREAM WAS ALIVE ON THE DAY, THEN DEAD

Daily livestreams drew strong views and engagement on the day. Afterwards they became largely dead, because few people rewatch a fifty minute live recording full of chat and off topic discussion.

Case 11 · Recommendation

FEWER LIVES, EDITED UPLOADS

Reduce raw livestream output and upload tightly edited recipe videos. Edited uploads attract new viewers and repeat views. Fewer lives also make the remaining lives feel more like an appointment.
