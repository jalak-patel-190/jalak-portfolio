Case 03 · Movies catalogue

Separate catalogue history from tenure results

How I operated a large existing Movies catalogue while keeping lifetime performance distinct from videos published during my tenure.

65.48M lifetime channel views 3.50M views from 89 tenure videos 5.4% of tracked views from tenure uploads

Context

A large catalogue was already in place

The channel had a substantial library before I joined. The challenge was to keep distribution active while reporting the inherited catalogue separately from the work published during my tenure.

Problem

A lifetime total can hide who published what

Using the full channel total as a personal achievement would overstate my contribution. The useful comparison is how many videos were already in the catalogue, how they performed, and what was published during my tenure.

Strategy

Report the catalogue in two time periods

I separated pre-tenure uploads from the videos published after I took over. That makes the existing channel scale visible without presenting inherited hits as work I created.

Evidence

Lifetime catalogue, separate from tenure work

The channel had 65,481,183 lifetime views in the reviewed snapshot. Most views came from videos published before my tenure, so the full channel total is context, not a personal result.

*The pre-tenure average excludes the 34,490,689-view outlier “Chudail Ne Rachaya Natak,” published in April 2025. It is not a result from my tenure.

One release during tenure

A fresh release published on 23 June 2026 reached approximately 1.40M views. It is a distinct tenure example, not a reason to attribute the channel’s inherited lifetime total to my work.

Results

The channel total is context, not a personal claim

The reviewed snapshot showed 65.48M lifetime views, including 3.50M views across 89 videos published during my tenure. The split is the result: a clearer account of the work without taking credit for inherited performance.

Takeaway

Attribution is part of the strategy

A channel can have impressive lifetime numbers and still require a careful account of what happened during a specific role. Separating publishing periods makes the case more credible and more useful to a hiring manager.
