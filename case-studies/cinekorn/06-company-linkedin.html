Case 06 · Company LinkedIn

Building Cinekorn's Industry Content Voice

Announcements are useful. They are not always a reason to keep following. The page needed a broader role: explain what is happening in the entertainment business.

+278% impressions/day
3.16% engagement rate
3.4x post frequency

Context

More than milestones

A company LinkedIn page can announce releases, partnerships, projects, and milestones. For Cinekorn, content could also explain industry dynamics, not only report what the company did.

Problem

News is cheap; explanation is scarce

The industry produces news daily. The hard part was choosing which topics deserved content and what Cinekorn could add. A simple update is available anywhere. A useful explanation needs another layer.

Strategy

Editorial structure

Focus areas included film distribution, cinema economics, OTT and digital media, international markets, release strategy, rights, and how films reach audiences.

Something happened → Why it matters → What sits underneath → Why industry should care

Instead of only "Cinekorn has done this," posts could ask "What does this change mean for the industry?" The company stayed in the story without turning every post into an ad.

Example posts: distribution after acquisition: 937 impressions, 18 clicks; a distribution deal: 889 impressions, 594 clicks; dubbing infrastructure: 484 impressions, 242 clicks. Objective was understanding, not only reactions.

Results

More consistent, more editorial

The page relied less on pure announcements and more on a broader reason to publish: Cinekorn's position inside the business of entertainment as a lens for explaining the industry.

Takeaway

What this showed

Company content does not always need to talk about the company. Sometimes the strongest presence comes from demonstrating what the organisation understands. For Cinekorn, that meant content about the business of entertainment, not only updates about the business.
