Case 11 · Context

WATCH HOURS WERE HIGH, BUT REVENUE WAS NOT FOLLOWING

After months of work, one problem stayed open. Watch hours were strong, but estimated revenue did not scale with them. I launched a YouTube membership as a new revenue lever, and the gap between watch hours and revenue remained.

Case 11 · Diagnosis

THE LIVESTREAM WAS ALIVE ON THE DAY, THEN DEAD

Daily livestreams drew strong views and engagement on the day. Afterwards they became largely dead, because few people rewatch a fifty minute live recording full of chat and off topic discussion.

FormatSame dayAfterwards
Raw livestreamStrong engagementFew rewatches
Edited recipe uploadBuilds over timeNew and repeat viewers

Case 11 · Recommendation

FEWER LIVES, EDITED UPLOADS

Recommendation. Reduce raw livestream output and upload tightly edited recipe videos. Edited uploads attract new viewers and repeat views. Fewer lives also make the remaining lives feel more like an appointment.
▶Example of an edited upload

Results

WHAT HAPPENED NEXT

The edited uploads started to work, but leadership paused the format before it had time to mature. My view is that any new content strategy needs about six months to train both the platform and the audience. Reverting early cut that process short.

I also proposed shooting livestreams and standalone videos together from one session. It was not implemented before I left.

What this showed

A STRATEGY NEEDS TIME TO PROVE ITSELF

The data showed the problem and the fix. Watching a strategy through its runway is the discipline that makes the next one more likely to work.
