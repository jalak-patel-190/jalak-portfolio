Solving a Data Bottleneck With AI-Assisted Tooling

Turning a repetitive 6,000 to 8,000-row classification task into a two-day workflow.

6K to 8KRows classified
3 to 4Source files
1K to 2KRows per file
2 daysTask completion
Context / Decision / Result

The Context

Early in the role, I was given a data-heavy classification task outside normal social media execution. Three to four files, each containing roughly 1,000 to 2,000 rows, needed to be sorted by tier.

The Problem

The data was usable, but the repetitive classification work created a bottleneck between raw information and actual strategic analysis. Doing the same operation thousands of times manually was not the best use of working time.

The Strategic Decision

I treated AI as a production tool, not as the decision-maker. The objective was to automate the repetitive processing while keeping the classification logic, review and final interpretation under human control.

The Execution

01Understand the classification logic and desired output.
02Use Gemini to help generate the Python workflow.
03Run the workflow in Google Colab.
The workflow accelerated the repetitive work. It did not replace the judgement used to check and interpret the output.

The Result

The classification task was completed in two days. I am deliberately not publishing a fabricated percentage of time saved because the original manual baseline was not formally recorded.

Evidence rule: the result is the documented task volume and completion time, not an invented efficiency percentage.

My Role

  • Identified the repetitive bottleneck.
  • Defined the classification requirement.
  • Selected the AI-assisted workflow.
  • Used Gemini to help generate the Python logic.
  • Ran the workflow in Google Colab.
  • Reviewed the output and used the processed data for strategy.

What This Changed

When a task becomes repetitive at scale, the useful question is not always how to do it faster manually. It can be whether it needs to remain manual at all.

Telugu and Kannada, Proof of Judgment Under Constraint

Working with weak source material without pretending it was premium content.

31Telugu videos
3,516Telugu lifetime views
98Kannada rebuilt-base videos
7,425Clean attributable Kannada views
Constraint / Packaging / Attribution

The Context

Telugu and Kannada entered the portfolio with weaker source material than the flagship channels. The available library included low-resolution prints, thin catalogs and uneven historical channel data.

The Problem

Better production alone could not solve a source-quality ceiling. Presenting older or low-resolution footage like a premium contemporary release would create an expectation the content could not meet.

The Strategic Decision

Instead of disguising the limitations, I adjusted the positioning and packaging around what the content actually was. Metadata and presentation could make the value of older regional cinema clearer before the click.

The Execution

  • Used positioning such as old movies, classic cinema and cult classic where appropriate.
  • Adjusted titles and descriptions to set a clearer viewer expectation.
  • Kept thumbnail treatment proportional to the quality of the source material.
  • Published consistently even when the available catalog was limited.

Telugu

The channel had zero recorded activity before 2026. After I took over, 31 videos were published and the channel recorded 3,516 lifetime views. Every one of those recorded views is from the post-takeover videos.

Kannada

Kannada shows 78,782 lifetime views, but 71,357 of those views came from pre-2026 content that is no longer in the current library. The cleaner attributable figure is 7,425 views across 98 videos published from the rebuilt base.

Attribution rule: the 78,782 figure is a channel lifetime number. The 7,425 figure is the cleaner number for the content published from the rebuilt base.

What This Proves

These channels are not presented as a large growth story. They demonstrate decision-making under a hard content constraint: understanding what can be improved, what cannot, and where effort should go.

My Role

  • Channel management and publishing.
  • Titles, descriptions and content positioning.
  • Thumbnail decisions based on source quality.
  • Performance interpretation and attribution analysis.

Baseline / Attribution / New Evidence

The Context

Cinekorn Movies is the flagship channel, with 65,481,183 lifetime views. Most of that performance, however, predates my tenure.

The Problem

A lifetime channel total can make an inherited catalog look like a current operator's result. I needed a clean baseline before making any performance claim.

What the Baseline Tells Me

Excluding the single outlier, the rest of the legacy catalog averages 67,021 views per video against 39,373 for my tenure uploads. That gap is the honest benchmark, not something to hide by quoting the 65M channel total alone.

Internal Comparison

Cinekorn Cineplex provides a cleaner tenure comparison. 61.1% of its tracked views came from the 55 videos I published, averaging 41,737 views each. The point is not that one channel is better than another. The point is that attribution becomes easier when the active content base is clearly separated.

My Role

  • Upload strategy and publishing cadence.
  • New-release packaging and channel management.
  • Catalog health and performance analysis.
  • Separating legacy performance from tenure performance.
  • Identifying meaningful new-release benchmarks.

Cinekorn Gujarati, A Cross-Platform Distribution System

Turning one licensed film into multiple platform-specific content products.

163Rakhewal pieces
17.77MRakhewal views
2.7×Original release multiple
6.42MRadha Tara re-release views
System / Proof / Recovery

The System

I treated a licensed film as source material for multiple audience-specific products rather than as one release event. YouTube carried full-length viewing, Facebook was used for mobile-first serial content, and Instagram was used for short, hooked scenes.

Proof: The Rakhewal Distribution Run

FormatVolumeViews
Fresh full release, Jan 1, 20261 video6,556,372
14-part serialised re-cut, June 202614 videos248,990
Shorts extraction run, June to September148 videos10,967,825
Total from tenure strategy163 pieces17,773,187

The 163 pieces generated 2.7 times the original full release's 6,556,372 views. I excluded a further 1.06M views on three pre-2026 Rakhewal uploads because they predate my involvement.

Proof: A Re-Release That Outperformed the Original

Radha Tara Vina Mane Gamtu Nathi had 3,785,146 lifetime views from its 2023 release. In July 2026, I split it into 15 parts and released one per day for two weeks. The 15 parts generated 6,423,457 views, 69.7% more than the original full film's lifetime views.

Facebook Recovery

Cinekorn Gujarati's Facebook page was restricted by Meta. The last post before the restriction was May 18, 2025, and the next post was March 25, 2026.

WindowPostsTotal ViewsApprox. Views / Day
2025 pre-restriction, 11 active weeks6526,325,860333,239
2026 post-recovery, 176 days43347,351,230269,041

The 2026 posting volume was 6.7 times the 2025 pre-restriction volume. Meta's dashboard reports a 41% year-over-year decline, but the comparison is affected by the short 2025 viral baseline and the subsequent ten-month blackout.

The Ventilator Funnel

I split Ventilator into 39 parts between April 20 and May 28, generating 4,015,837 views. When viewers asked what film a clip came from or where to watch the full version, I answered with the YouTube link. The comment section became a manual bridge from short-form discovery to full-length viewing.

What I Am Still Diagnosing

Average views per post fell from roughly 104,000 to 23,000 between July and August while posting volume roughly halved. The original restriction cause also remains unconfirmed. I am keeping both points visible rather than inventing explanations.

My Role

  • Content distribution strategy across YouTube and Facebook.
  • Format selection and sequencing.
  • Publishing and performance analysis.
  • Manual comment-to-YouTube routing.
  • Catalog and re-release decisions.

Editorial / Cadence / Organic Evidence

The Context

I have managed the Cinekorn Entertainment company page since April 13, 2026. The editorial focus is distribution, cinema economics, OTT, international markets and the business decisions behind entertainment content.

The Writing Approach

The content follows a simple editorial sequence: something happened, why it matters, what is underneath it, and why an entertainment professional should care. The goal is to add an explanation rather than simply reposting an announcement.

Post-Level Examples

The account-level numbers are supported by individual content examples. A July 6 post recorded 937 impressions, 18 clicks, 8 likes and 2 reposts. A July 17 post recorded 889 impressions and 594 clicks. A June 24 post recorded 484 impressions and 242 clicks.

Metric clarification: LinkedIn's exported CTR is clicks divided by impressions. It should not be described as an engagement rate. High click ratios are therefore reported as CTR where used.

The Strategic Shift

The page moved toward useful industry explanations: distribution mechanics, market signals, release timing, OTT windows and operational details that entertainment professionals can learn from.
