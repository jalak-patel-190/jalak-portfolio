Case 03 · Movies catalog

Managing a Movies catalog without claiming inherited hits

Operate a large existing film library while separating historical channel performance from the work published during my tenure.

65.5M lifetime channel views 411 legacy videos 89 tenure videos

Context

A large library with an established history

The Movies channel already had a substantial back catalogue before I took over. The task was to keep distribution active while reporting clearly on what existed before my tenure and what was published during it.

Problem

Lifetime views do not equal personal contribution

A lifetime channel total can hide the difference between inherited performance and current work. Treating the full number as a result of my tenure would overstate the contribution and make the case study less credible.

Decision

Separate channel history from tenure performance

I kept the lifetime figure as context for the channel's scale, then assessed tenure publishing separately. That distinction makes the work easier to evaluate without taking credit for older hits.

Evidence

The Movies channel as an operating environment

The channel's lifetime views describe the size of the catalogue, not a result attributable entirely to my tenure. The analysis separates the existing library from the videos published during the period I managed the channel.

Result

A clearer account of the work

The case distinguishes the channel's historical scale from the videos published during my tenure. This gives a more accurate picture of the operating work without attributing inherited hits to new activity.

Takeaway

Credible reporting starts with clear attribution

When managing a mature content library, separate what was already there from what changed under current management. That is the foundation for evaluating the work honestly.
