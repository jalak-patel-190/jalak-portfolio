Case 01 · Ajmera Fashion

A LANGUAGE IS NOT AN AUDIENCE

Around fifty accounts, each with its own region, vocabulary and product taste. This case covers how the team kept them distinct, and the pieces I owned.

50 accounts15 on the keyword roster7 search phrases I owned

Context

A WHOLESALER THAT NEEDED BUYERS TO TRUST IT

Ajmera Fashion sells sarees and traditional clothing wholesale to small retailers and first time entrepreneurs across India. Most of those buyers live in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, and they decide on trust before price. YouTube was where that trust started.

When I joined, the company already ran a large set of accounts split by language, by product and by business model. I was a fresher in my first digital marketing role, working inside a team led by my team leader.

Problem

THE SAME VIDEO DOES NOT WORK IN EVERY LANGUAGE

A video in Marathi does not reach a Marathi buyer if the product, the search terms and the framing do not match what that region looks for. A Maharashtrian buyer searches for a Paithani, not a “silk saree”. An Odia buyer looks for Sambalpuri Ikat, not a “cotton saree”.

Around fifty accounts, each needing its own product mix and its own vocabulary, is a systems problem before it is a content problem.

Strategy

GIVE EVERY REGION ITS OWN MAP

The team built the system in four parts. I worked inside it and owned specific pieces of it.

Language map
Title formula
Keyword roster
Script library
  • Language map. Every language channel had its own product mix, with an informative track beside it.
  • Title formula. A product or price hook, plus the market or city, plus a trust word such as Manufacturer, Wholesaler or Factory, written in Hindi and English together.
  • Keyword roster. Fifteen people each owned a cluster of search phrases to rank for.
  • Script library. Business idea Shorts scripts, ready made quote posts in Gujarati and Odia, and a topic list for the founder’s video series.

Execution

WHAT I DID INSIDE THE SYSTEM

Channel product mix

I read YouTube Analytics to see which products pulled views on which channel, then moved each channel’s mix toward them. The Marathi channel leaned into silk sarees and the pieces that go with them, blouse, petticoat, astar and fall. Gujarati leaned into daily wear, Gandhi pattern and chiffon sarees. Odia leaned into cotton sarees and nighties.

Regional titles

The same logic applied to titles. Naming the specific regional product, Paithani or Karpabardi in Maharashtra, Sambalpuri Ikat in Odisha, got better views than the generic “silk saree” or “cotton saree”.

▶Maharashtra: Paithani and Karpabardi▶Odisha: Sambalpuri Ikat

My seven search phrases

Fifteen people on the team each owned a cluster of search phrases. Mine were aimed at buyers looking for cheap and wholesale sarees from Surat.

  1. Surat me sabse sasti saree kaha pe milti hai?
  2. Surat thok ke bhavse Kapde kaha se Milege?
  3. Surat Saree Wholesaler
  4. Surat Saree Manufacturer
  5. Sasti Sareeya Kaha Milti Hai?
  6. Sabse sasti Saree Surat
  7. Surat Cheapest Saree Near Me

Everything around it

Planning and posting schedules, scripts and festive content, keeping designers aligned with the plan, and community posts such as festival greetings and quizzes in the community tab.

Results

THE REGIONAL CHANNELS MOVED FASTEST

Between 15 December 2022 and 15 June 2023, Telugu grew 32.4% and Tamil grew 31.6%, against 10.9% for the main channel. The regional channels started from smaller bases, so this shows momentum, not size.

The tracker cannot tell me which video or title drove the difference, so I do not claim a cause. The pattern fits the strategy, but it does not prove it.

What this showed

PER CHANNEL BEATS PER PORTFOLIO

Across the whole portfolio, growth looked modest. Read channel by channel, the regional channels stood out. That is the habit I took from this role: look at each audience on its own before judging the whole.
